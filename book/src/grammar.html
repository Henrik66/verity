Verity's grammar is Python 3.12's grammar (Python Language Reference, section 10) with the changes below, in the notation of About These Docs. [[settled]] Rule names follow Python's grammar where they exist.

Changed rules

(* Two new atoms. *)
atom          ::= (* Python's atoms *) | "verity" | "falsity"

(* because sits between not and and. *)
conjunction   ::= because_test ("and" because_test)*
because_test  ::= inversion ["because" inversion]
inversion     ::= "not" inversion | comparison

(* Quantifiers are expressions with the lowest precedence. *)
expression    ::= quantifier | (* Python's expression *)
quantifier    ::= ("forall" | "exists") NAME "in" disjunction "," expression

Unchanged rules with new meaning

assert_stmt   ::= "assert" expression ["," expression]   (* raises TestimonyError *)
comparison    ::= bitwise_or (compare_op bitwise_or)*     (* at most one compare_op until chains are settled *)

Keywords

Python's keywords, plus verity, falsity, because, forall and exists (Section 2.5).

Notes

  • In a call, f(forall x in xs, p, y) passes two arguments: the quantifier (body p) and y. The body is an expression, not an expression list, so it stops at the next comma.
  • In because_test, at most one because is permitted (E0202).
  • A chained comparison is syntactically a comparison with several operators. The compiler rejects it with E0201 (Section 4.3).
  • True and False parse as names and are rejected after parsing with E0101, so the diagnostic can carry a fix.
