# Botanical illustration references

These are plates, seed catalogs and field guides that could help with Verity's sky-blue meadow and the paper section below the fold. Each entry says what to borrow for the web page, not just why it looks nice. All links loaded on 2026-10-26. Some primary hosts (BHL, Kew, British Museum) block automated fetches, so a stable mirror or encyclopedia page is linked in their place.

1. **Burpee's Farm Annual, 1894 (W. Atlee Burpee & Co., USDA NAL Gilbert Collection)**
   https://archive.org/details/burpeesfarmannua1894watl
   Transfer: the seed-catalog voice. Big lithographed cover vegetables on a plain ground, then dense pages where each variety gets a small engraved vignette, a bold name and a short blurb. This fits a below-the-fold "feature packets" grid with one small drawing per card.

2. **Henry G. Gilbert Nursery and Seed Trade Catalog Collection (USDA National Agricultural Library), cover exhibit**
   https://www.nal.usda.gov/exhibits/speccoll/exhibits/show/nursery-and-seed-trade-catalog
   Transfer: covers that put one round emblem (such as the 1890 Gardiner "Japanese Lilies" roundel) next to a plain block of name, company and address. Our yellow sphere could work the same way, as a medallion beside a calm type block, so the art and the text don't fight.

3. **Vilmorin-Andrieux, *Les fleurs de pleine terre* (Paris, 1866)**
   https://archive.org/details/lesfleursdeplein00vilm_0
   Transfer: small black-and-white wood engravings set into the text column, with a lot of off-white page around them. This shows how one-colour line drawings in our ink `#243e30` could punctuate long docs on `#f7f6ec` paper without full-bleed imagery.

4. **Ernst Haeckel, *Kunstformen der Natur* (1899–1904), all 100 plates in order**
   https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Kunstformen_der_Natur
   Transfer: a strict "number. Latin name" caption system ("82. Hepaticae; Marchantia") and symmetric plates that cluster many specimens around a centre. Use it for numbered section headers, and maybe a radial arrangement of small flowers around the smiley. Don't copy the ornamental density.

5. **Mary Delany, *Flora Delanica* paper mosaics (1771–c.1783, 985 works, British Museum)**
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_Delany
   Transfer: flat cut-paper shapes with no outlines, shaded by stacking lighter and darker pieces of the same hue. This is almost exactly our SVG approach of filled silhouettes in layered greens, and it argues for keeping shapes flat and layered rather than adding gradients. Her single specimen on a dark field is also a strong pattern for one hero plant on a dark footer.

6. **Iwasaki Kan'en, *Honzō Zufu* (Iconographia Plantarum, woodblock, 1828 onward)**
   https://onlinebooks.library.upenn.edu/webbin/who/Iwasaki%2c%20Kan%27en%2c%201786%2d1842 (links to HathiTrust page scans)
   Transfer: a woodblock plant that fills the page from edge to edge and runs off it, with flat colour areas and a short vertical text column tucked into the empty corner. This is a model for letting the meadow bleed off the hero while a tiny label sits in the open sky.

7. **Herbarium sheet anatomy (Natural History Museum of Utah, "Decoding a Herbarium Sheet")**
   https://nhmu.utah.edu/articles/decoding-herbarium-sheet
   Transfer: a fixed sheet grammar, with the specimen floating in white space, a ruler along the edge, a colour bar at the top, an institution stamp plus barcode, and the data block always in the lower right. This maps well onto code-sample cards: the snippet is the specimen, metadata goes in the bottom-right label, and thin "paper tape" corner details are optional.

8. **Kristyna Baczynski, *The Wild Year* (TarcherPerigee, 2022)**
   https://penguinrandomhouse.com/books/702736/the-wild-year-by-kristyna-baczynski
   Transfer: a modern hand-drawn field guide organised by season, with more than 90 specimens drawn in a friendly line, plus checklists and blank space for notes. It shows that a warm, slightly naive line can live next to a cheerful character without turning childish. A seasonal structure could also suit release notes or a "getting started" path.
   - Alternative contemporary line: **Lizzie Harper**, https://www.lizzieharper.co.uk/. Her watercolour and graphite plates show precise botanical drawing that stays light, which is useful if a "specimen of the release" spot illustration is ever wanted.
