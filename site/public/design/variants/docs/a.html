Testimony Semanticssection 5 of 10

Abstract

This section specifies the testimony carried by truths: the node kinds, how each operator constructs them, how testimony.explain renders them, and when the compiler may omit them.

5.1. Node kinds

A testimony is a tree of nodes. The kinds are:

5.2. Construction rules

Below, T(e) is the testimony of a truth e, and V(e) its value.

5.2.3. Conjunction

For p1 and p2 and ... and pn, evaluated left to right:

(a)If some pi is a falsity, let k be the first such index. Then V = falsity and T = T(pk). The operands after pk are not evaluated. [settled]
(b)Otherwise V = verity and T = all(T(p1), ..., T(pn)). A child that is itself an all node produced directly by and is spliced in place. A because node is never flattened. [settled]

5.3. Rendering

Example, from the conformance suite (examples/validate.ity):

def valid_username(name) =
    (len(name) >= 3 because f"{name!r} has at least 3 characters")
    and (len(name) <= 16 because f"{name!r} has at most 16 characters")
    and (string_ops.is_alnum(name) because f"{name!r} is alphanumeric")

print(explain(valid_username("al")))

Output:

falsity
  refuted: 'al' has at least 3 characters
    len(name) >= 3: 2 < 3

The first line is verity or falsity. A node at depth d is one line indented by 2*d spaces, followed by its children at depth d + 1.
